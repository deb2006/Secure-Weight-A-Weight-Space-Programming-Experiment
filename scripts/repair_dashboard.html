Can an LLM repair a neural network?

We gave an LLM the task and the numbers inside a classifier. Could it find damaged connections and restore predictions with at most four edits?

The inspiration

MIT CSAIL’s experiments asked whether LLMs can read and write neural weights. SecureWeight asks whether they can repair a trained classifier from a single checkpoint. Takehi et al., 2026

The testbed

Two small neural networks

Both use one hidden layer and three class scores. Rows stay fixed across the three training seeds for each dataset.

LLM under study GPT-6, as identified in the experiment conversations.

Exploratory phase

What happened in the pilot?

Five scored cases tested sign reversal, erasing the hidden layer, and damage that left the output weights looking plausible.

Pilot scorecard

Correct predictions per pilot case and network state

Iris · three replacements

Three of 40 erased parameters reactivated two neurons through petal length and width. All seven validation predictions preserved by the fault also survived the patch. On held-out flowers, 15 errors were corrected and one was introduced.

Wine · four replacements

Four edits restored 18/18 correct validation predictions. Held-out accuracy rose from 11/27 to 25/27, two short of the healthy network. Both subtle pilot cases received KEEP, followed by NO when asked about damage.

Blinded paired study

Twelve checkpoints under review

Each of six trained networks supplied a healthy copy and a partner with three altered output weights. Prompts were shuffled across fresh conversations.

One-shot decision

The LLM could keep the checkpoint, express uncertainty or propose up to four scalar replacements. No examples, scores, repair tools or healthy reference were supplied.

Follow-up diagnosis

The same conversation then answered whether it suspected damage and whether it could specify a patch. Only edits from the original reply were scored on the held-out set.

Inference

Finding a fault and fixing it are different outcomes.

Across the twelve blinded checkpoints, the initial replies proposed three edits. Each targeted an altered weight. All three replacement values differed from their healthy originals. On Wine trial 06, two replacements corrected four held-out errors. On Iris trial 12, a correctly located sign change left every evaluated prediction unchanged. The other four damaged checkpoints received KEEP.

Three of six damaged checkpoints drew suspicion. All three positive follow-ups declined to specify an edit. The six healthy controls drew no false alarms.

Study design, selection and limits

Three training seeds per dataset shared one split. Faults affected three of 24 output weights. Replacement values lay between the healthy layer’s 5th and 95th percentiles and cost two or three correct validation predictions. Held-out scores played no role in fault selection. This small batch measures behavior on these cases; performance across other faults, networks and repeated LLM responses remains open.

Trial 03 received repeated follow-up wording within a single message. Trial 11’s first completed response followed an interrupted generation and a retry. Earlier blank pilot replies were recorded as missing.
